Lesson 5

Layers

Build your design step by step. Organize, stack, and edit elements without affecting the whole image.

What Are Layers?

Layers are one of the most fundamental concepts in Photoshop. Think of them as transparent sheets stacked on top of each other — each layer can hold different content like images, text, shapes, or adjustments, and you can edit each one independently without affecting the others.

The Layers Panel

The Layers Panel (Window → Layers) shows all your layers in a stack. The layer at the top of the list appears in front of layers below it. You can:

  • Click a layer to select and edit it
  • Drag layers to reorder them
  • Click the eye icon to show or hide a layer
  • Double-click a layer name to rename it
  • Click the lock icon to prevent accidental edits
💡

Tip: Use descriptive names for your layers. Organized layers save a huge amount of time on complex projects.

Creating & Managing Layers

Create a new blank layer with Ctrl+Shift+N (Cmd+Shift+N). Duplicate a layer by pressing Ctrl+J (Cmd+J) — this is one of the most-used shortcuts in Photoshop. Delete a layer by selecting it and pressing the trash icon in the Layers Panel.

Layer Groups & Blending Modes

Group related layers together using Ctrl+G (Cmd+G) to keep your panel tidy. You can also change a layer's Blending Mode (the dropdown at the top of the Layers Panel) to control how it interacts with the layers below — modes like Multiply, Screen, and Overlay are used constantly in photo editing.

⚠️

Important: Never edit the original image layer directly. Duplicate it first (Ctrl+J) so you always have a backup.
