What is Photoshop?

Learn what Adobe Photoshop is and how it's used to edit photos and create designs. A quick intro for beginners starting out in digital design.

Overview

Adobe Photoshop is a raster graphics editor developed and published by Adobe Inc. It is considered the industry standard tool for photo editing, digital painting, and graphic design. First launched in 1988, it has grown into one of the most powerful and widely used creative tools in the world.

Whether you're retouching a portrait, compositing a movie poster, or designing a social media graphic, Photoshop gives you the tools to bring any visual idea to life.

💡

Beginner tip: You don't need to know every tool to get started. Focus on the basics first and build your skills one layer at a time.

What Can Photoshop Do?

Photoshop is used across many creative industries. Here are some common uses:

  • Photo retouching and color correction
  • Digital painting and illustration
  • Graphic design for print and web
  • Photo compositing and manipulation
  • UI and web design mockups
  • 3D rendering and texturing

Raster vs. Vector

Photoshop is primarily a raster editor, meaning it works with pixels. This is different from vector-based software like Adobe Illustrator, which uses mathematical paths. Photoshop images can lose quality when scaled up, so always work at the correct resolution from the start.

⚠️

Good to know: Always work with high-resolution files (at least 300 DPI for print, 72 DPI for web) to ensure your designs look sharp.

Why Learn Photoshop?

Photoshop is one of the most in-demand creative skills today. From photographers and graphic designers to marketers and content creators, knowing Photoshop opens doors across many career paths. It is also the foundation for learning other Adobe tools like Illustrator, InDesign, and After Effects.
